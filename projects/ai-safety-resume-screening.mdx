## Overview

Developed as part of the **CS427 AI Safety** module (AY 2025/2026 Term 2), this project evaluates ChatGPT and other large language models when used as automated resume screening systems — a high-stakes application that directly affects people's job opportunities.

Although ChatGPT is a general-purpose LLM, it is increasingly used in professional contexts to evaluate resumes, rank candidates, and generate hiring recommendations. We treat it as a black-box screening tool that receives resumes as input and produces candidate scores and shortlists.

**Status:** Work in progress.

---

## Motivation

AI-assisted hiring systems are increasingly used by companies to filter applicants, often before any manual review takes place. However, ChatGPT was not originally designed for resume screening, raising safety concerns around fairness and the potential for manipulation. Small variations in wording, formatting, or demographic details may influence model outputs in unexpected ways.

Through initial testing, we discovered that AI models may artificially inflate the score of a mediocre minority candidate over an identical majority candidate — a form of **protective leniency** where the model attempts to fulfill a learned fairness quota. This is likely a byproduct of extensive alignment phases intended to counter historical biases, resulting in unintended over-corrections during evaluation.

## Safety Dimensions Evaluated

### 1. Fairness
- **Demographic Parity:** Comparing average scores across different demographic groups
- **Individual Fairness:** Scoring identical resumes that differ only in demographic characteristics (counterfactual pairs)

### 2. Robustness
- Sensitivity to formatting changes, wording variations, and resume length
- Consistency across repeated runs (stochastic variation analysis)

### 3. Model Interpretability & Hallucination
- Analysing rationale quality for candidate selections
- Detecting hallucinations in reasoning traces and false assumptions about candidates

### 4. Privacy
- Dependence on personal identifiers: measuring score changes when sensitive information is anonymised
- Exposure risks: whether the model unnecessarily repeats or emphasises sensitive details

## Evaluation Methodology

We adopted two complementary evaluation approaches reflecting real-world hiring workflows:

### Workflow 1: Resume Scoring (Absolute Judgement)

The model assigns a numerical score (0–100) to a single resume under controlled conditions:
- Fixed system prompt instructing the model to act as a strict technical recruiter
- Consistent job description used throughout all experiments
- Counterfactual resumes differing only in a single controlled attribute (e.g., name)
- Multiple runs to account for stochastic variation

**Early findings from test experiments:**
| Model | Non-Minority Score | Minority Score |
|---|---|---|
| GPT-OSS:20B | 18/100 | 25/100 |
| Mistral:7B | 50/100 | 65/100 |

### Workflow 2: Candidate Selection (Relative Judgement)

The model evaluates paired and multi-candidate resumes using forced-choice decisions to detect systematic preference patterns. We hypothesise that when forced to choose between identically underqualified resumes, models may select minority candidates regardless and engage in **post-hoc rationalisation** — hallucinating false structural reasons to hide demographic preference.

### Cross-Model Testing

- **Closed models** (ChatGPT, Claude, DeepSeek): evaluated via OpenRouter as an LLM gateway
- **Open-source models** (Mistral, LLaMA): run locally via Ollama to minimise costs

## Proposed Mitigations

### 1. Pre-Processing Anonymisation
A mandatory pre-processing script that strips all names and pronouns before the LLM scores a resume. Implementation options range from simple regex to a PII-BERT model combined with a basic GenAI anonymiser — directly building on techniques from our [PII Anonymisation project](https://shariffrashid.com/projects/pii-anonymisation-gen-ai).

### 2. Model Fine-Tuning
Fine-tuning a model with a LoRA adapter to improve PII detection and de-bias resume scoring, with before/after evaluation of fairness metrics.
